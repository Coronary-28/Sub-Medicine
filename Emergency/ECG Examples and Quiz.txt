Which leads are used for rapid ECG-axis determination?
A) II and III
B) aVR and aVL
C) I and aVF
D) V1 and V6
E) III and aVL
Correct Answer: C) I and aVF
Explanation: Leads I and aVF divide the frontal plane into quadrants (I positive and aVF positive = normal axis; I positive and aVF negative = leftward axis; I negative and aVF positive = right axis). Lead II is used to resolve borderline left axis deviation.
###
How many large squares are there in 1 second of an ECG tracing at the standard paper speed of 25 mm/s?
A) 5
B) 3
C) 10
D) 25
E) 1
Correct Answer: A) 5
Explanation: One large square equals 0.20 s, so 1 second contains 5 large squares (25 mm). A small square equals 0.04 s.
###
What is the heart rate on the regular ECG strip shown, in which each R–R interval spans two large squares? [img: ERECG150beats.png ]
A) 80
B) 100
C) 130
D) 150
E) 180
Correct Answer: D) 150
Explanation: Heart rate = 300 ÷ number of large squares between consecutive R waves = 300 ÷ 2 = 150 beats/min.
###
How many large squares are there in 1 minute of ECG tracing?
A) 150
B) 300
C) 600
D) 1500
E) 60
Correct Answer: B) 300
Explanation: At 25 mm/s, 1 second contains 5 large squares, so 1 minute contains 5 × 60 = 300. This is the basis of the 300 rule for heart rate.
###
How many small squares are there in 1 minute of ECG tracing?
A) 300
B) 600
C) 750
D) 3000
E) 1500
Correct Answer: E) 1500
Explanation: One small square equals 0.04 s, so 60 ÷ 0.04 = 1500. This is the basis of the 1500 rule for precise rate calculation.
###
How many small squares correspond to 1 mV of amplitude on a standard ECG?
A) 5
B) 2.5
C) 10
D) 15
E) 25
Correct Answer: C) 10
Explanation: The standard calibration is 10 mm/mV, and each small square (1 mm) equals 0.1 mV, so 10 small squares equal 1 mV.
###
Which method is used to calculate the heart rate from a regular rhythm on an ECG?
A) 300 divided by the number of large squares between consecutive R waves
B) 1500 divided by the number of large squares between consecutive R waves
C) 300 divided by the number of small squares between consecutive R waves
D) The number of QRS complexes in one large square multiplied by 300
E) The R–R interval in seconds multiplied by 60
Correct Answer: A) 300 divided by the number of large squares between consecutive R waves
Explanation: The 300 rule applies to regular rhythms. The 1500 rule requires small squares (1500 ÷ small squares), and the heart rate is 60 divided by the R–R interval in seconds, not multiplied by it.

###
What ECG findings define right bundle branch block? [img: ERECGRBBB.png ]
A) QRS ≥120 ms with broad notched R waves in I, aVL, and V5–V6 and a deep S wave in V1
B) QRS ≥120 ms with an rsR′ (M-shaped) pattern in V1–V2 and a broad terminal S wave in I and V6
C) QRS <120 ms with an rSR′ pattern in V1 and normal terminal forces
D) Short PR interval with a delta wave and a widened QRS
E) Constant PR interval with intermittent non-conducted P waves
Correct Answer: B) QRS ≥120 ms with an rsR′ (M-shaped) pattern in V1–V2 and a broad terminal S wave in I and V6
Explanation: RBBB requires a QRS ≥120 ms with late right-ventricular forces (rsR′ in V1–V2) and a broad terminal S wave in the left-sided leads. Option C describes incomplete RBBB, and option A describes LBBB.
###
What diagnostic difficulties can arise when a patient has LBBB on the ECG?
A) It abolishes P waves, preventing rhythm interpretation
B) It shortens the PR interval and mimics pre-excitation
C) It produces diffuse concave ST elevation with PR depression, mimicking pericarditis
D) It produces a dominant R wave in V1, mimicking posterior MI
E) It causes secondary ST–T changes that can obscure or mimic acute ST elevation, making MI diagnosis and localisation difficult
Correct Answer: E) It causes secondary ST–T changes that can obscure or mimic acute ST elevation, making MI diagnosis and localisation difficult
Explanation: LBBB alters ventricular depolarisation and repolarisation, so ischaemic ST changes are hard to interpret. Clinical context, serial biomarkers, and validated criteria such as modified Sgarbossa are used, and a new LBBB with chest pain is managed as a STEMI equivalent.
###
Which set of ECG findings supports the diagnosis of right ventricular hypertrophy?
A) Sokolow-Lyon voltage ≥35 mm with left axis deviation and lateral strain pattern
B) Left axis deviation with deep S waves in V1 and tall R waves in V5–V6
C) Right axis deviation with a dominant R wave in V1 (R/S ratio >1), with or without ST depression and T-wave inversion in V1–V3
D) Tall R wave in aVL with ST depression and T-wave inversion in I and aVL
E) Pathological Q waves in V1–V4 with ST elevation
Correct Answer: C) Right axis deviation with a dominant R wave in V1 (R/S ratio >1), with or without ST depression and T-wave inversion in V1–V3
Explanation: RVH is recognised by right axis deviation and a dominant R wave in V1 (R/S >1), often with a right ventricular strain pattern. Option A describes LVH.
###
What are the ECG characteristics of left bundle branch block?
A) QRS ≥120 ms; broad notched or slurred R waves in I, aVL, and V5–V6; absent septal q waves; deep S waves in V1–V3; secondary ST–T discordance
B) QRS ≥120 ms; rSR′ pattern in V1; broad terminal S wave in I and V6
C) QRS <120 ms with a delta wave and short PR interval
D) QRS ≥120 ms with a terminal R wave in aVR >3 mm
E) Narrow QRS with a tall R wave in V1 and ST depression in V1–V3
Correct Answer: A) QRS ≥120 ms; broad notched or slurred R waves in I, aVL, and V5–V6; absent septal q waves; deep S waves in V1–V3; secondary ST–T discordance
Explanation: LBBB shows a broad monophasic or notched R wave in the lateral leads and a deep QS or rS in V1–V3, with ST–T changes opposite to the QRS direction. Option B describes RBBB, and option D describes TCA toxicity.
###
A patient with chest pain has a normal ECG and elevated cardiac troponin. This most likely suggests:
A) STEMI
B) Stable angina
C) Unstable angina
D) NSTEMI
E) Acute pericarditis
Correct Answer: D) NSTEMI
Explanation: NSTEMI is myocardial infarction with a raised troponin and no ST elevation (the ECG may be normal, or show T inversion or ST depression). Unstable angina is the closest alternative, but its troponin is normal.
###
A patient with suspected inferior MI has ST elevation in leads II, III, and aVF and in lead V4R. Which drug is contraindicated?
A) Aspirin
B) Nitroglycerin
C) Heparin
D) Beta-blocker
E) Atorvastatin
Correct Answer: B) Nitroglycerin
Explanation: ST elevation in V4R indicates right ventricular infarction, which is preload-dependent, so nitrates can cause profound hypotension. Nitrates are also contraindicated after recent PDE-5 inhibitor use.
###
A patient has an unstable tachyarrhythmia with a pulse. What is the immediate treatment?
A) Adenosine
B) Amiodarone
C) Synchronized cardioversion
D) Atropine
E) Unsynchronized defibrillation
Correct Answer: C) Synchronized cardioversion
Explanation: An unstable tachyarrhythmia with a pulse requires immediate synchronized cardioversion, with sedation if feasible. Adenosine is for stable regular narrow-complex tachycardia, and unsynchronized defibrillation is for pulseless VT or VF.
###
What rhythm is shown on the ECG strip? [img: ERECGMAT.png ]
A) Sinus tachycardia
B) Atrial flutter
C) Multifocal atrial tachycardia
D) Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome
E) Atrial fibrillation
Correct Answer: C) Multifocal atrial tachycardia
Explanation: MAT is an irregular tachycardia with at least three distinct P-wave morphologies and variable PR intervals. Atrial fibrillation is the closest alternative, but it has no discernible P waves.
###
The ECG shows constant PR intervals with intermittent non-conducted P waves and dropped QRS complexes. What is the diagnosis? [img: ERECGsecAVblockM2.png ]
A) Second-degree AV block, Mobitz type II
B) Second-degree AV block, Mobitz type I (Wenckebach)
C) First-degree AV block
D) Third-degree (complete) AV block
E) Sinoatrial exit block
Correct Answer: A) Second-degree AV block, Mobitz type II
Explanation: A constant PR interval before a suddenly dropped QRS defines Mobitz II. Mobitz I shows progressive PR lengthening before the dropped beat.
###
An ECG shows diffuse concave (saddle-shaped) ST elevation in all leads. What is the diagnosis and treatment? [img: ERECGpericarditis.png ]
A) STEMI — urgent PCI
B) Benign early repolarization — no treatment
C) Hyperkalaemia — calcium gluconate
D) Acute pericarditis — NSAID (ibuprofen)
E) Acute pulmonary embolism — heparin
Correct Answer: D) Acute pericarditis — NSAID (ibuprofen)
Explanation: Diffuse concave ST elevation with PR depression that does not follow a coronary territory indicates pericarditis, treated with an NSAID plus colchicine. STEMI shows convex elevation in contiguous leads with reciprocal depression.
###
ST-segment elevation in leads II, III, and aVF indicates infarction of which wall?
A) Anterior wall
B) Inferior wall
C) Lateral wall
D) Posterior wall
E) Septal wall
Correct Answer: B) Inferior wall
Explanation: Leads II, III, and aVF face the inferior wall of the left ventricle, typically supplied by the right coronary artery.
###
A haemodynamically stable patient has a regular monomorphic wide-complex tachycardia (ventricular tachycardia). Which treatment is most appropriate? [img: ERECGamiodarone.png ]
A) Atropine
B) Verapamil
C) Unsynchronized defibrillation
D) Synchronized cardioversion
E) IV amiodarone
Correct Answer: E) IV amiodarone
Explanation: Stable monomorphic VT is treated with IV antiarrhythmics (amiodarone, procainamide, lidocaine, or sotalol). Synchronized cardioversion is reserved for unstable VT, and defibrillation for pulseless VT.
###
Which of the following describes the ECG characteristics of sinus arrhythmia?
A) Irregularly irregular rhythm with absent P waves
B) Inverted P waves before every QRS with a shortened PR interval
C) Normal P waves before every QRS with a constant PR interval and P–P variation of at least 120 ms, classically respiratory
D) Progressive PR lengthening with intermittently dropped QRS complexes
E) Regular P–P intervals with variable QRS morphology
Correct Answer: C) Normal P waves before every QRS with a constant PR interval and P–P variation of at least 120 ms, classically respiratory
Explanation: In sinus arrhythmia the P, QRS, and T morphology is normal and only the R–R timing varies, rising with inspiration and falling with expiration. Non-respiratory variation suggests pathology.
###
Which characteristic ST-segment morphology is seen with digoxin effect?
A) Scooped (sagging) ST-segment depression
B) Diffuse concave ST elevation with PR depression
C) Coved ST elevation in V1–V2
D) Osborn waves at the J point
E) Peaked T waves with a widened QRS
Correct Answer: A) Scooped (sagging) ST-segment depression
Explanation: Digoxin effect produces a scooped ST depression ("reverse tick") with a shortened QT, and is a benign finding at therapeutic levels. Digoxin toxicity instead causes arrhythmias.
###
A report states "possible left axis deviation" (lead I positive, aVF negative). Which finding confirms true left axis deviation?
A) Lead II positive
B) Lead III positive
C) Lead aVR negative
D) Lead II negative
E) Lead aVL negative
Correct Answer: D) Lead II negative
Explanation: When lead I is positive and aVF is negative, a negative lead II confirms true left axis deviation (about −30° to −90°). A positive lead II means the axis is borderline and may still be normal.
###
Which ECG findings suggest an early repolarization pattern?
A) Convex ST elevation in contiguous leads with reciprocal ST depression and evolving Q waves
B) J-point elevation ≥1 mm in at least two contiguous leads with concave ST elevation and a notched or slurred QRS downstroke
C) Coved ST elevation in V1–V2 with a pseudo-RBBB pattern
D) Diffuse concave ST elevation with PR depression
E) Scooped ST depression with a shortened QT interval
Correct Answer: B) J-point elevation ≥1 mm in at least two contiguous leads with concave ST elevation and a notched or slurred QRS downstroke
Explanation: Early repolarization is a benign pattern, most common in young males and athletes, that is stable on serial ECGs. Pericarditis is the closest alternative, but it shows PR depression and diffuse changes.
###
A patient has first-degree heart block on ECG (PR >200 ms, all P waves conducted). What is the immediate management?
A) Atropine
B) Permanent pacemaker insertion
C) Synchronized cardioversion
D) Transcutaneous pacing
E) No acute treatment needed
Correct Answer: E) No acute treatment needed
Explanation: First-degree AV block is a benign finding in which every impulse is conducted, so no treatment is needed unless it is symptomatic or drug-induced.
###
Which of the following is NOT a feature of a pathological Q wave?
A) Width ≥0.04 s (one small square)
B) Depth >25% of the following R-wave amplitude
C) Width <0.04 s
D) Depth ≥2 mm (0.2 mV)
E) Presence in leads V1–V4
Correct Answer: C) Width <0.04 s
Explanation: A pathological Q wave is ≥40 ms wide and deeper than 25% of the R wave. A Q wave narrower than 40 ms is physiological, and any Q wave in V1–V4 is considered pathological.
###
All of the following shorten the QT interval EXCEPT:
A) Hypothyroidism
B) Hypercalcaemia
C) Hyperkalaemia
D) Digoxin effect
E) Hyperthermia
Correct Answer: A) Hypothyroidism
Explanation: Hypothyroidism prolongs the QT interval (via bradycardia). Hypercalcaemia, hyperkalaemia, digoxin effect, and increased sympathetic tone (fever, hyperthyroidism) shorten it.
###
An ECG shows an irregularly irregular rhythm with absent P waves. What is the diagnosis?
A) Atrial flutter with variable block
B) Multifocal atrial tachycardia
C) Ventricular fibrillation
D) Atrial fibrillation
E) Sinus arrhythmia
Correct Answer: D) Atrial fibrillation
Explanation: AF shows chaotic atrial activity with no P waves, a fibrillatory baseline, and an irregularly irregular ventricular response. MAT is also irregular, but it has at least three distinct P-wave morphologies.
###
Which option correctly defines a pathological Q wave and lists conditions that can produce pseudoinfarction Q waves?
A) Width <40 ms and depth <25% of the R wave; causes: MI, WPW, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, LBBB
B) Width ≥40 ms and depth >25% of the R wave; causes: prior MI, WPW, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, LBBB
C) Width ≥40 ms only; causes: hyperkalaemia, hypothermia, digoxin effect, cardiac tamponade
D) Depth ≥2 mm in leads III and aVR only; causes: PE, pericarditis, early repolarization, Brugada syndrome

Correct Answer: B) Width ≥40 ms and depth >25% of the R wave; causes: prior MI, WPW, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, LBBB
Explanation: Pathological Q waves represent myocardial scar. Pseudoinfarction Q waves occur in WPW (negative delta wave), hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, LBBB, severe LVH, and acute myocarditis.
###
Which group of conditions causes shortening of the QT interval?
A) Hypocalcaemia, hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia, hypothermia
B) Class Ia/III antiarrhythmics, macrolides, antipsychotics, ondansetron
C) Hypothyroidism, bradycardia, raised ICP, myocardial ischaemia
D) Congenital long QT syndrome, hypomagnesaemia, tricyclic antidepressants
E) Hypercalcaemia, hyperkalaemia, digoxin effect, congenital short QT syndrome
Correct Answer: E) Hypercalcaemia, hyperkalaemia, digoxin effect, congenital short QT syndrome
Explanation: These conditions shorten repolarisation. The other options list classic causes of QT prolongation (electrolyte depletion, drugs, and bradycardic states).
###
P mitrale (a broad, notched P wave >0.12 s in lead II) is most commonly associated with:
A) COPD
B) Pulmonary hypertension
C) Mitral stenosis (left atrial enlargement)
D) Pulmonary fibrosis
E) Right ventricular hypertrophy
Correct Answer: C) Mitral stenosis (left atrial enlargement)
Explanation: P mitrale reflects left atrial enlargement, as in mitral stenosis, mitral regurgitation, or left heart failure. COPD, pulmonary fibrosis, and pulmonary hypertension cause the tall peaked P pulmonale instead.
###
Which of the following is a recognised cause of poor R-wave progression across the precordial leads?
A) Anteroseptal myocardial infarction
B) Posterior myocardial infarction
C) Type A WPW (left-sided accessory pathway)
D) Normal variant in young children
E) Isolated right bundle branch block
Correct Answer: A) Anteroseptal myocardial infarction
Explanation: Loss of anterior forces from anteroseptal infarction (also LVH, LBBB, COPD, and lead misplacement) impairs R-wave progression. Posterior MI, left-sided WPW, RBBB, and the paediatric variant produce tall R waves in V1.
###
Which of the following is a recognised cause of a tall R wave in V1?
A) Anteroseptal myocardial infarction
B) Left bundle branch block
C) COPD with emphysema
D) Posterior myocardial infarction
E) Left ventricular hypertrophy
Correct Answer: D) Posterior myocardial infarction
Explanation: Posterior infarction produces a tall R wave in V1–V2 as the mirror image of posterior Q waves. Other causes are RVH, RBBB, left-sided WPW, and dextrocardia. The other options cause poor R-wave progression or deep S waves in V1.
###
An ECG shows both a tall peaked P wave and a broad notched P wave. Which chamber-enlargement pattern does this suggest?
A) Right atrial enlargement only
B) Biatrial enlargement
C) Left atrial enlargement only
D) Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome
E) Atrial fibrillation
Correct Answer: B) Biatrial enlargement
Explanation: The tall peaked component reflects right atrial enlargement (P pulmonale) and the broad notched component reflects left atrial enlargement (P mitrale), so both atria are enlarged.
###
What are the normal duration and amplitude of the P wave on ECG?
A) <80 ms and <1 mm
B) <120 ms and <5 mm
C) <200 ms and <2.5 mm
D) >120 ms and <2.5 mm
E) <120 ms (3 small squares) and <2.5 mm in limb leads
Correct Answer: E) <120 ms (3 small squares) and <2.5 mm in limb leads
Explanation: A normal P wave is shorter than 120 ms and lower than 2.5 mm in the limb leads. A longer P wave suggests left atrial enlargement, and a taller one suggests right atrial enlargement.
###
At a paper speed of 25 mm/s, what defines a narrow and a wide QRS complex?
A) Narrow <80 ms; wide ≥80 ms
B) Narrow <100 ms; wide ≥100 ms
C) Narrow <120 ms (<3 small squares); wide ≥120 ms (≥3 small squares)
D) Narrow <200 ms; wide ≥200 ms
E) Narrow <120 ms; wide >200 ms
Correct Answer: C) Narrow <120 ms (<3 small squares); wide ≥120 ms (≥3 small squares)
Explanation: A QRS of 120 ms or more is wide, suggesting a ventricular origin, bundle branch block, or pre-excitation.
###
Which of the following is NOT a cause of QT prolongation?
A) Hypercalcaemia
B) Hypokalaemia
C) Hypomagnesaemia
D) Hypocalcaemia
E) Hypothermia
Correct Answer: A) Hypercalcaemia
Explanation: Hypercalcaemia shortens the QT interval. Hypocalcaemia, hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia, and hypothermia prolong it, as do many drugs.
###
Which of the following is a recognised cause of left axis deviation?
A) Right ventricular hypertrophy
B) Left posterior fascicular block
C) COPD
D) Left anterior fascicular block
E) Pulmonary embolism
Correct Answer: D) Left anterior fascicular block
Explanation: Left axis deviation (lead I positive, aVF negative) occurs with left anterior fascicular block, LVH, inferior MI, and ventricular pacing. The other options cause right axis deviation.
###
In which limb lead are the P wave, QRS complex, and T wave normally predominantly negative?
A) Lead I
B) Lead aVR
C) Lead II
D) Lead III
E) Lead aVL
Correct Answer: B) Lead aVR
Explanation: Lead aVR looks at the heart from the right shoulder, opposite to the normal direction of depolarisation, so all its waves are normally negative.
###
When can the normally negative complexes in lead aVR become predominantly positive?
A) Right ventricular hypertrophy
B) Posterior myocardial infarction
C) Left bundle branch block
D) Pulmonary embolism
E) Dextrocardia or right-arm/left-arm limb-lead reversal
Correct Answer: E) Dextrocardia or right-arm/left-arm limb-lead reversal
Explanation: Both conditions invert the normal limb-lead vectors. They are distinguished by precordial R-wave progression, which is lost in dextrocardia and normal in limb-lead reversal.
