A 30-year-old woman presents with chest pain. She has a history of previous deep vein thrombosis, is not pregnant, and pulmonary embolism is suspected. What is the investigation of choice?
A) Chest X-ray
B) Echocardiography
C) CT pulmonary angiography (CTPA)
D) 12-lead ECG
E) Ventilation-perfusion (V/Q) scan
Correct Answer: C) CT pulmonary angiography (CTPA)
Explanation: CTPA is the first-line confirmatory test for suspected PE in a non-pregnant patient with no contrast contraindication. V/Q scanning is preferred only in pregnancy, significant renal impairment, or contrast allergy, and CXR, ECG, and echocardiography cannot confirm PE.

A patient presents with tearing chest pain and a difference in blood pressure between the two arms. What is the best medication to give initially?
A) Labetalol
B) Aspirin
C) Alteplase
D) Sublingual nitroglycerin
E) Unfractionated heparin infusion
Correct Answer: A) Labetalol
Explanation: Tearing chest pain with an inter-arm BP differential suggests aortic dissection, which requires IV beta-blockade first to lower heart rate and aortic shear stress. Vasodilators are added only after heart-rate control, and antiplatelets, anticoagulants, and thrombolytics are contraindicated.
###
A diabetic patient presents with dyspnoea but no chest pain. Which diagnosis must NOT be excluded?
A) Costochondritis
B) Gastro-oesophageal reflux disease
C) Acute pericarditis
D) Acute myocardial infarction
E) Viral pleurisy
Correct Answer: D) Acute myocardial infarction
Explanation: Diabetic patients, especially with autonomic neuropathy, often present with silent or atypical ACS in which dyspnoea is an anginal equivalent. The other options are pain-predominant conditions and are not the critical diagnosis to exclude here.
###
An ECG shows ST-segment elevation in leads II, III and aVF. The most likely location of the myocardial infarction is:
A) Anterolateral
B) Inferior
C) Septal
D) Posterior
E) Lateral
Correct Answer: B) Inferior
Explanation: ST elevation in II, III and aVF localises the infarct to the inferior wall (usually RCA), often with reciprocal depression in I and aVL. Lateral infarcts involve I, aVL, V5–V6, and anterior or anterolateral infarcts involve the precordial leads.
###
Chest pain radiating to the jaw and relieved by sitting forward is most likely due to:
A) Stable angina
B) Gastro-oesophageal reflux disease
C) Pulmonary embolism
D) Acute myocardial infarction
E) Acute pericarditis
Correct Answer: E) Acute pericarditis
Explanation: Pericarditis pain is classically worse when lying supine and relieved by sitting forward, and it may radiate to the neck, back, or jaw. Stable angina is exertional and relieved by rest or GTN, not by posture.
###
Chest pain that is reproducible on palpation of the chest wall most likely indicates:
A) Acute myocardial infarction
B) Unstable angina
C) Costochondritis (musculoskeletal chest pain)
D) Acute pericarditis
E) Aortic dissection
Correct Answer: C) Costochondritis (musculoskeletal chest pain)
Explanation: Pain reproduced by chest-wall palpation points to a musculoskeletal cause and lowers the probability of ACS (LR ≈ 0.3). It does not exclude ACS on its own, since about 15% of ACS patients also have chest-wall tenderness.
###
Which of the following correctly distinguishes between the three types of angina?
A) Stable angina: predictable exertional pain relieved by rest or GTN; unstable angina: new, crescendo, or rest pain without troponin rise; vasospastic (Prinzmetal) angina: episodic rest pain from coronary spasm with transient ST elevation
B) Stable angina: rest pain with transient ST elevation; unstable angina: predictable exertional pain relieved by rest; vasospastic angina: pain with troponin rise
C) Stable angina: pain at rest from plaque rupture; unstable angina: exertional pain relieved by rest; vasospastic angina: exertional pain treated first-line with beta-blockers
D) All three types are characterised by elevated troponin from myocardial necrosis and differ only in the duration of pain
E) Unstable angina differs from stable angina by a raised troponin, and vasospastic angina is treated first-line with beta-blockers
Correct Answer: A) Stable angina: predictable exertional pain relieved by rest or GTN; unstable angina: new, crescendo, or rest pain without troponin rise; vasospastic (Prinzmetal) angina: episodic rest pain from coronary spasm with transient ST elevation
Explanation: Stable angina is demand ischaemia on a fixed plaque, and unstable angina is plaque rupture with partial thrombosis but normal troponin; a troponin rise defines NSTEMI. Vasospastic angina is treated with calcium channel blockers or nitrates, and beta-blockers are generally avoided.
###
All of the following are life-threatening causes of chest pain EXCEPT:
A) Aortic dissection
B) Tension pneumothorax
C) Pulmonary embolism
D) Costochondritis
E) Oesophageal rupture (Boerhaave's syndrome)
Correct Answer: D) Costochondritis
Explanation: Costochondritis is a benign musculoskeletal cause of chest pain. Aortic dissection, tension pneumothorax, PE, and oesophageal rupture are all immediately life-threatening causes.
###
A patient with a previous history of myocardial infarction attends the cardiology clinic for a routine check-up. He is asymptomatic. His ECG shows a prolonged PR interval with 1:1 AV conduction. What is the most appropriate management?
A) Atropine 0.5 mg IV
B) No acute therapy
C) Transcutaneous pacing
D) Permanent pacemaker insertion
E) Epinephrine infusion
Correct Answer: B) No acute therapy
Explanation: A fixed PR interval above 200 ms with 1:1 conduction is first-degree AV block, which is benign and needs no treatment when asymptomatic. Pacing is reserved for symptomatic bradycardia, asymptomatic Mobitz II, or complete heart block.
###
What is the first-line anti-inflammatory treatment for uncomplicated acute pericarditis?
A) Oral corticosteroids alone
B) NSAID alone
C) Paracetamol with opioid analgesia
D) Colchicine alone
E) NSAID (e.g. ibuprofen) plus colchicine, with gastroprotection
Correct Answer: E) NSAID (e.g. ibuprofen) plus colchicine, with gastroprotection
Explanation: Colchicine added to an NSAID reduces symptom duration and the recurrence rate. Corticosteroids are reserved for when NSAIDs and colchicine are contraindicated or fail, and aspirin is preferred in post-MI pericarditis.

###
An ECG shows diffuse concave ST-segment elevation with PR-segment depression. What is the most appropriate management?
A) Aspirin or NSAIDs plus colchicine
B) Activation of the cath lab for primary PCI
C) Intravenous fibrinolytic therapy
D) Intravenous heparin infusion
E) High-dose oral corticosteroids as first-line therapy
Correct Answer: A) Aspirin or NSAIDs plus colchicine
Explanation: Diffuse concave ST elevation with PR depression indicates acute pericarditis, not coronary occlusion. Reperfusion therapy is for STEMI with regional convex ST elevation and reciprocal changes, and corticosteroids are second-line.
###
Diffuse ST-segment elevation with PR-segment depression is seen in:
A) Cardiac tamponade
B) Pulmonary embolism
C) Acute anterior STEMI
D) Acute pericarditis
E) Benign early repolarisation
Correct Answer: D) Acute pericarditis
Explanation: Pericarditis causes diffuse concave ST elevation with PR depression (and PR elevation in aVR). Tamponade is characterised by low-voltage QRS and electrical alternans, and benign early repolarisation lacks PR depression and is stable on serial ECGs.
###
How is a posterior myocardial infarction recognised on the standard 12-lead ECG, and how is it best confirmed?
A) ST elevation in V1–V3, confirmed by right-sided leads (V4R)
B) ST depression in V1–V3 with tall broad R waves, confirmed by ST elevation in posterior leads V7–V9
C) ST elevation in II, III and aVF, confirmed by right-sided leads (V4R)
D) Pathological Q waves in V1–V3, confirmed by right-sided leads (V3R–V4R)
E) ST elevation in I, aVL, V5–V6, confirmed by posterior leads V7–V9
Correct Answer: B) ST depression in V1–V3 with tall broad R waves, confirmed by ST elevation in posterior leads V7–V9
Explanation: Posterior MI appears on the standard ECG only as reciprocal changes in V1–V3, and ST elevation in the posterior leads (V7–V9) gives direct confirmation. Right-sided leads (V4R) are used to confirm right ventricular infarction.
###
Which medication should be avoided in inferior myocardial infarction because of possible right ventricular infarction and preload dependence?
A) Aspirin
B) Oxygen
C) Morphine
D) Clopidogrel
E) Nitroglycerin
Correct Answer: E) Nitroglycerin
Explanation: Nitrates reduce preload, which can cause severe hypotension in a preload-dependent RV infarction (also after recent PDE-5 inhibitor use). Aspirin and antiplatelet therapy remain indicated, and oxygen is given only when SpO₂ is below 95%.
