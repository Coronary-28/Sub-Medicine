Which of the following is the primary treatment goal in a patient with sinus tachycardia?
A) Administer adenosine
B) Prepare for synchronized cardioversion
C) Identify and treat the underlying cause
D) Administer amiodarone
E) Administer a beta-blocker
Correct Answer: C) Identify and treat the underlying cause
Explanation: Sinus tachycardia is a compensatory physiological response (fever, pain, hypovolaemia, anxiety), and the rate settles once the cause is treated. Adenosine and cardioversion are used for reentrant or unstable tachyarrhythmias, and suppressing a compensatory tachycardia can be harmful.

After delivering a defibrillation shock to a patient in VF/pulseless VT, what is the IMMEDIATE next step?
A) Resume CPR immediately, starting with chest compressions, for 2 minutes
B) Check for a carotid pulse
C) Check the rhythm on the monitor
D) Administer epinephrine
E) Deliver a second shock
Correct Answer: A) Resume CPR immediately, starting with chest compressions, for 2 minutes
Explanation: Compressions are resumed without delay after the shock, and the rhythm is reassessed only at the end of the 2-minute cycle. A pulse is checked only if an organized rhythm or signs of ROSC appear.

D) Sodium bicarbonate

Correct Answer: B) Magnesium sulfate
Explanation: Torsades de pointes is treated with IV magnesium sulfate regardless of the serum magnesium level. Amiodarone and procainamide prolong the QT interval and can worsen it, and sodium bicarbonate is reserved for hyperkalaemia, TCA overdose and severe acidosis.

Correct Answer: E) Fibrinolytic therapy
Explanation: Fibrinolytics lyse the occluding thrombus in eligible STEMI when PCI cannot be delivered in time. Aspirin and heparin are antithrombotic adjuncts that do not restore flow, and epinephrine and atropine have no reperfusion role.

B) Peripheral
C) Intraosseous
D) Endotracheal
E) Intracardiac

Which listed medication is NOT used in the adult algorithm for persistent VF or pulseless VT?
A) Atropine
B) Amiodarone
C) Lidocaine
D) Epinephrine
Correct Answer: A) Atropine
Explanation: Persistent VF/pulseless VT is managed with repeated defibrillation, high-quality CPR, epinephrine, and amiodarone or lidocaine. Atropine is used for symptomatic bradycardia and has no role in shockable arrest. Magnesium sulfate is reserved for pulseless torsades de pointes, so it was removed from the options to avoid ambiguity.

What are the proper steps for operating an AED?
A) Power on, attach electrode pads, shock the patient, analyze the rhythm
B) Power on, analyze the rhythm, attach electrode pads, shock the patient
C) Power on, shock the patient, attach electrode pads, analyze the rhythm
D) Power on, attach electrode pads, analyze the rhythm, shock the patient
E) Attach electrode pads, shock the patient, power on, analyze the rhythm
Correct Answer: D) Power on, attach electrode pads, analyze the rhythm, shock the patient
Explanation: The AED needs power and attached pads before it can analyze the rhythm, and a shock is delivered only if analysis finds a shockable rhythm.
###
How is refractory ventricular fibrillation managed in the adult cardiac arrest algorithm?
A) Stop CPR and give epinephrine alone
B) Continue high-quality CPR with repeated defibrillation, epinephrine every 3–5 minutes, and amiodarone or lidocaine (typically after the third shock), while treating reversible causes
C) Switch to synchronized cardioversion
D) Give atropine as the primary antiarrhythmic
E) Give adenosine
Correct Answer: B) Continue high-quality CPR with repeated defibrillation, epinephrine every 3–5 minutes, and amiodarone or lidocaine (typically after the third shock), while treating reversible causes
Explanation: Drugs are added to, and never replace, CPR and defibrillation. Synchronized cardioversion is for rhythms with a pulse, and atropine and adenosine have no role in VF.
###
What does AED stand for?
A) Automatic Energy Delivery
B) Automated External Device
C) Autonomous Energy Defibrillator
D) Automatic External Delivery
E) Automated External Defibrillator
Correct Answer: E) Automated External Defibrillator
Explanation: An AED analyzes the cardiac rhythm and delivers a shock if a shockable rhythm is detected.
###
What is the first step after a shockable rhythm is detected on the defibrillator?
A) Administer amiodarone
B) Check the pulse
C) Deliver the shock and immediately resume CPR
D) Administer epinephrine
E) Perform endotracheal intubation
Correct Answer: C) Deliver the shock and immediately resume CPR
Explanation: The shock is delivered promptly and CPR resumed at once, without pausing for a pulse check or drug administration.
###
When should the rhythm be rechecked after a shock delivered by an AED?
A) After one 2-minute cycle of CPR, or whenever the AED prompts a rhythm analysis
B) Immediately after the shock
C) After 5 minutes
D) After 30 compressions
E) After two rescue breaths
Correct Answer: A) After one 2-minute cycle of CPR, or whenever the AED prompts a rhythm analysis
Explanation: CPR resumes immediately after the shock and the rhythm is reassessed about every 2 minutes. A pulse is checked only if an organized rhythm or signs of ROSC appear, and CPR is not paused after every 30 compressions.

Where are standard anterolateral AED pads placed?
A) Both pads on the anterior chest, side by side
B) Right lower chest and left upper chest
C) Directly over the sternum, one above the other
D) Right upper chest (below the right clavicle) and left lower lateral chest (below the left armpit)
E) Both pads on the left lateral chest
Correct Answer: D) Right upper chest (below the right clavicle) and left lower lateral chest (below the left armpit)
Explanation: This placement directs the current through the heart. A pad must not be placed directly over an implanted device, and a wet chest must be dried.

Which of the following, by itself, is NOT a sign of haemodynamic instability in a patient with atrial flutter?
A) Ischaemic chest discomfort
B) A high ventricular rate alone
C) Hypotension
D) Altered mental status
E) Acute pulmonary oedema
Correct Answer: B) A high ventricular rate alone
Explanation: Instability is defined by hypotension or shock, altered consciousness, ischaemic chest pain and acute pulmonary oedema. The rate must be interpreted together with perfusion and symptoms.
###
Which of the following is NOT a sign of instability in a patient with a tachyarrhythmia?
A) Hypotension
B) Altered mental status
C) Cold, clammy skin
D) Ischaemic chest pain
E) Asymptomatic hypertension
Correct Answer: E) Asymptomatic hypertension
Explanation: Instability features are hypotension, decreased level of consciousness, signs of shock, severe chest pain and pulmonary oedema (acute heart failure). Hypertension alone, without symptoms, is not a sign of instability.

All of the following are true regarding paediatric versus adult airway and chest anatomy EXCEPT:
A) The airway is narrowest at the subglottic/cricoid area
B) The cervical spine is relatively straight
C) The thoracic cage is rigid with well-developed accessory muscles
D) The ribs are more horizontal and breathing depends mainly on the diaphragm
E) The tongue and occiput are relatively larger
Correct Answer: C) The thoracic cage is rigid with well-developed accessory muscles
Explanation: The paediatric chest wall is compliant, with horizontal ribs and poorly developed accessory muscles, so children rely on the diaphragm. This is why retractions appear early in respiratory distress.
###
Which of the following is NOT a standard intraosseous (IO) access site?
A) Neck of the femur
B) Proximal tibia
C) Distal femur
D) Proximal humerus
E) Distal tibia
Correct Answer: A) Neck of the femur
Explanation: Standard IO sites are the proximal tibia (most common first choice), distal tibia, proximal humerus, and the distal femur (paediatric only). The femoral neck is not an IO insertion site.
###
You are alone with a patient in suspected cardiac or respiratory arrest. After checking for danger and before sending for help, what is the next step?
A) Start chest compressions
B) Call an ambulance
C) Open the airway
D) Check for response
E) Check the carotid pulse
Correct Answer: D) Check for response
Explanation: After scene safety, responsiveness is assessed. The rescuer then shouts for help or activates the emergency response and checks breathing and pulse together for no more than 10 seconds.
###
All of the following are true regarding confirmation of endotracheal tube (ETT) position EXCEPT:
A) Auscultation
B) Visualizing the tube passing through the oesophagus
C) Continuous waveform capnography (ETCO2)
D) Chest X-ray
E) Direct visualization of the tube passing through the vocal cords
Correct Answer: B) Visualizing the tube passing through the oesophagus
Explanation: A tube passing into the oesophagus is a misplacement, not a confirmation. Correct placement is confirmed by passage through the vocal cords and, most reliably, by continuous waveform capnography together with clinical checks.
###
Which of the following is NOT a contraindication to intraosseous line insertion?
A) Fracture of the target bone
B) Previous IO attempt in the same bone
C) Infection (osteomyelitis or cellulitis) over the insertion site
D) Osteogenesis imperfecta
E) Fluid leakage from the line
Correct Answer: E) Fluid leakage from the line
Explanation: Leakage is a post-insertion complication (extravasation or malposition), not a pre-insertion contraindication, and the line should be stopped and another site chosen. The other options are contraindications to placement.
###
Which of the following is an absolute contraindication to IV thrombolysis in acute ischaemic stroke?
A) Major surgery 3 weeks ago
B) Pregnancy
C) Ischaemic stroke within the last 3 months
D) Minor or rapidly improving symptoms
E) Seizure at onset with residual post-ictal deficits
Correct Answer: C) Ischaemic stroke within the last 3 months
Explanation: Ischaemic stroke or serious head trauma within the previous 3 months is an absolute exclusion. Recent major surgery, pregnancy, minor or rapidly improving symptoms, and seizure at onset with residual deficits are relative cautions.
