Which of the following is NOT a mechanism of carbon monoxide toxicity?
A) Decreases oxygen delivery to the tissues
B) Binds reversibly to haemoglobin with an affinity 200–230 times that of oxygen
C) Increases oxygen transport to the tissues
D) Shifts the oxygen–haemoglobin dissociation curve to the left
E) Binds to cytochrome oxidase and impairs oxidative metabolism
Correct Answer: C) Increases oxygen transport to the tissues
Explanation: CO forms carboxyhaemoglobin, which lowers oxygen-carrying capacity and left-shifts the curve, so oxygen transport and release both fall. It never increases them.
###
All of the following are NOT true regarding CO poisoning in pregnancy EXCEPT, which statement is true? Which statement is NOT true regarding CO poisoning in pregnancy?
A) Elimination of CO from the fetus is faster than from the mother
B) CO crosses the placenta freely
C) Fetal haemoglobin binds CO with a higher affinity than adult haemoglobin
D) The fetus is more susceptible to CO than the mother
E) Fetal COHb rises more slowly but eventually reaches a higher level than the mother's
Correct Answer: A) Elimination of CO from the fetus is faster than from the mother
Explanation: CO is eliminated from the fetus much more slowly than from the mother, which is why the fetus ends up with higher COHb and is more susceptible to hypoxia even when the mother survives.
###
Carbon monoxide poisoning affects the fetus in all of the following ways EXCEPT:
A) Fetal haemoglobin has a higher affinity for carbon monoxide
B) Carbon monoxide is eliminated slowly from the fetal circulation
C) Fetal tissues become hypoxic
D) Carbon monoxide is rapidly eliminated from the fetus
E) Fetal COHb rises more slowly but ends higher than the maternal level
Correct Answer: D) Carbon monoxide is rapidly eliminated from the fetus
Explanation: Elimination from the fetus is slow, not rapid. Combined with the higher affinity of fetal Hb for CO, this prolongs fetal hypoxia.
###
Which of the following is the WRONG mechanism regarding carbon monoxide poisoning?
A) Carbon monoxide binds haemoglobin with high affinity
B) It increases oxygen release from haemoglobin to the tissues
C) It left-shifts the oxygen–haemoglobin dissociation curve
D) It causes tissue hypoxia
E) It decreases the oxygen-carrying capacity of the blood
Correct Answer: B) It increases oxygen release from haemoglobin to the tissues
Explanation: CO increases the affinity of the remaining haem sites for oxygen (left shift), so oxygen release to the tissues decreases, not increases.
###
What is the mechanism of action of carbon monoxide?
A) Inhibition of acetylcholinesterase
B) Oxidation of ferrous iron in haemoglobin to form methaemoglobin
C) Right shift of the dissociation curve with increased oxygen release
D) Haemolysis of red blood cells
E) Binding to haemoglobin with 200–230 times the affinity of oxygen, forming carboxyhaemoglobin and reducing oxygen-carrying capacity
Correct Answer: E) Binding to haemoglobin with 200–230 times the affinity of oxygen, forming carboxyhaemoglobin and reducing oxygen-carrying capacity
Explanation: CO competes with O₂ at the haem site and forms COHb, which lowers carrying capacity and left-shifts the curve, causing tissue hypoxia. AChE inhibition is the organophosphate mechanism, and methaemoglobin is formed by oxidation, not by CO.
###
All of the following can cause CO poisoning EXCEPT:
A) Car exhaust fumes
B) Gas heater
C) Electrical heater
D) Charcoal fire
E) House fire smoke
Correct Answer: C) Electrical heater
Explanation: CO comes only from incomplete combustion of carbon-containing fuels. An electrical heater involves no combustion and produces no CO.
###
Which of the following is NOT a source of carbon monoxide?
A) Electrical heater
B) Firearm discharge (cartridge)
C) Motorcycle exhaust
D) Charcoal burning
E) Methylene chloride vapours (paint remover)
Correct Answer: A) Electrical heater
Explanation: An electrical heater has no combustion, so it produces no CO. Firearm discharge gases contain CO, and methylene chloride is metabolised by the liver to CO.
###
Which of the following is NOT a risk factor for CO poisoning?
A) Poorly ventilated or enclosed space
B) Exercise or increased metabolic demand
C) Low oxygen or high altitude
D) Decreased ambient temperature
E) Cigarette smoking
Correct Answer: D) Decreased ambient temperature
Explanation: Increased ambient temperature, not decreased, raises the rate of CO uptake. Cold is not a risk factor.
###
All of the following are features of carbon monoxide gas and its poisoning EXCEPT:
A) Colourless and odourless
B) Causes irritative symptoms on inhalation
C) Tasteless
D) The most common type of fatal poisoning in many countries
E) Produced by incomplete combustion of fuels
Correct Answer: B) Causes irritative symptoms on inhalation
Explanation: CO is non-irritant, so there are no warning symptoms, which is why it is called the "silent killer".
###
CO poisoning is associated with all of the following EXCEPT:
A) Headache
B) Cherry-red skin
C) Confusion
D) Sinus tachycardia and ST-segment changes on ECG
E) Cyanosis
Correct Answer: E) Cyanosis
Explanation: Despite asphyxiation, cyanosis does not occur in CO poisoning because COHb is bright red; the skin is pink or pale and the lips are bright red.
###
Cherry-red discoloration after CO poisoning is located in:
A) Skin and mucous membranes only
B) Muscles only
C) Skin, mucous membranes, muscles, and internal organs
D) Internal organs only
E) Blood only
Correct Answer: C) Skin, mucous membranes, muscles, and internal organs
Explanation: Carboxyhaemoglobin and carboxymyoglobin colour the blood, skin, lividity, mucous membranes, muscles and internal organs, so no single site is the answer.
###
What is the normal COHb level in the blood of a resting healthy non-smoker?
A) 1–3%
B) 10%
C) 20%
D) 60%
E) 90%
Correct Answer: A) 1–3%
Explanation: Background COHb from endogenous haem catabolism is the lowest option, about 1–3% (0.4–0.7% in the lecture). Smokers reach about 5–10%, and symptoms usually begin around 10–20%.
###
In a death from CO poisoning, the cherry-pink colour is absent. This implies that:
A) The cause of death was not CO poisoning
B) The death was caused by hanging
C) The patient had methaemoglobinaemia
D) The patient was resuscitated
E) The patient died too quickly for any COHb to form
Correct Answer: D) The patient was resuscitated
Explanation: Resuscitation and oxygen administration lower the COHb level, so the cherry-pink colour fades. Its absence does not exclude CO, and a blood COHb level confirms the diagnosis.
###
What is the mainstay of management of CO poisoning?
A) Dialysis
B) 100% oxygen therapy
C) Naloxone
D) Pralidoxime
E) Atropine
Correct Answer: B) 100% oxygen therapy
Explanation: High-flow 100% O₂ shortens the half-life of CO from 3–4 hours on room air to 30–90 minutes, and hyperbaric O₂ shortens it further. Pralidoxime and atropine are for organophosphate poisoning, and naloxone is for opioids.
###
All of the following cause a LEFT shift of the oxygen–haemoglobin dissociation curve EXCEPT:
A) Alkalosis
B) Hypothermia
C) Decreased 2,3-DPG
D) Fetal haemoglobin
E) Increased CO₂ and acidosis
Correct Answer: E) Increased CO₂ and acidosis
Explanation: Acidosis and increased CO₂ cause a RIGHT shift (Bohr effect), which lowers Hb affinity and increases O₂ release. The other four increase affinity and shift the curve to the left.
###
All of the following are risk factors for a LEFT shift of the oxygen–haemoglobin dissociation curve EXCEPT:
A) Alkalosis
B) Decreased 2,3-DPG
C) Respiratory acidosis
D) Decreased temperature
E) Carbon monoxide
Correct Answer: C) Respiratory acidosis
Explanation: Respiratory acidosis (high CO₂, low pH) right-shifts the curve through the Bohr effect, which favours O₂ release. All the other options left-shift it.
