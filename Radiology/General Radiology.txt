Which of the following is usually hyperdense on non-contrast CT?
A) Fat
B) Cerebrospinal fluid
C) Bone
D) Chronic blood
E) Air
Correct Answer: C) Bone
Explanation: Bone, acute blood, calcification, metal, and the lens are hyperdense on non-contrast CT. Fat, CSF, chronic blood, and air are hypodense.

Reducing the kV from 90 to 70 in a radiograph, with image density maintained, will generally:
A) Increase patient exposure
B) Decrease iodine–soft tissue contrast
C) Decrease calcium–soft tissue contrast
D) Allow a decrease in mAs
E) Decrease the contrast between air and soft tissue
Correct Answer: A) Increase patient exposure
Explanation: Lowering kV reduces beam penetration, so mAs must be increased to keep the same image density, and patient dose rises. Contrast between tissues improves rather than falls at lower kV.
