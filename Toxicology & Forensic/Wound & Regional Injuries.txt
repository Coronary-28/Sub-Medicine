In a suicidal attempt, which finding is most characteristic?
A) Multiple deep wounds scattered over the body
B) Accidental injury
C) Hesitation marks
D) Defence wounds
E) Chop wounds from a heavy weapon
Correct Answer: C) Hesitation marks
Explanation: Hesitation marks are multiple superficial, parallel, non-lethal cuts made before the fatal cut. Defence wounds, by contrast, are found on the victim of an attack, on the dorsum of the hand and the forearm.
###
Which of the following is characteristic of a suicidal cut throat in a right-handed person?
A) Hesitation cuts, with the wound starting high on the left side and running obliquely downward, deepest at its start
B) Multiple deep, scattered wounds with defence injuries on the hands
C) A deep horizontal wound with no hesitation cuts
D) Incised wounds on the back of the neck
E) A wound starting on the right side of the neck and deepest at its end
Correct Answer: A) Hesitation cuts, with the wound starting high on the left side and running obliquely downward, deepest at its start
Explanation: A suicidal cut throat is accessible, is grouped on the side opposite the dominant hand, and is accompanied by hesitation cuts. A homicidal cut is deep, often horizontal, without hesitation cuts, and may show defence wounds.
###
The green colour of a bruise is due to:
A) Oxyhaemoglobin
B) Haemosiderin
C) Bilirubin
D) Biliverdin
E) Deoxyhaemoglobin
Correct Answer: D) Biliverdin
Explanation: Green appears at about 3–5 days and is due to biliverdin. Brown (1–3 days) is haemosiderin, and yellow (5–7 days) is bilirubin.
###
Which of the following statements about abrasions is FALSE?
A) They are superficial wounds involving the epidermis
B) Their healing is characterized by granulation tissue formation rather than re-epithelialisation
C) They may indicate the direction of the applied force
D) They heal without scarring
E) They may reproduce the pattern of the causative object
Correct Answer: B) Their healing is characterized by granulation tissue formation rather than re-epithelialisation
Explanation: Abrasions involve the epidermis only, so they heal by re-epithelialisation from the edges and hair follicles under a scab, leaving no scar.
###
Which of the following is TRUE about abrasions?
A) They always heal with a scar
B) They cannot be dated
C) They are produced only by sharp instruments
D) They involve the full thickness of the skin
E) Even if they bleed slightly, they heal without scarring
Correct Answer: E) Even if they bleed slightly, they heal without scarring
Explanation: Abrasions are epidermal injuries, and even when they bleed a little from the papillary dermis they re-epithelialise without a scar. They are caused by blunt force, not sharp instruments.
###
Which type of injury gives the best indication of the object or weapon used?
A) Laceration
B) Incised wound
C) Patterned abrasion
D) Brush abrasion
E) Deep bruise
Correct Answer: C) Patterned abrasion
Explanation: A patterned abrasion reproduces the surface texture of the causative object (tyre tread, grille, buckle, teeth). A brush abrasion shows only the direction of sliding, and a laceration does not reflect the weapon's dimensions.
###
What is the importance of Langer's lines (skin cleavage lines) in wound assessment?
A) Determining the degree of gaping of the wound
B) Determining the age of the wound
C) Estimating the time of death
D) Differentiating ante-mortem from post-mortem wounds
E) Identifying the type of weapon
Correct Answer: A) Determining the degree of gaping of the wound
Explanation: Langer's lines follow the direction of dermal collagen and elastic fibres. A wound across the lines gapes widely, whereas a wound parallel to them gapes little and heals with a finer scar.
###
Crescent-shaped (semilunar) abrasions are caused by:
A) Ligature
B) Teeth
C) A ring
D) Fingernail
E) A fall on a rough surface
Correct Answer: D) Fingernail
Explanation: A fingernail pressed into the skin leaves a small crescentic abrasion, or a linear scratch if dragged. Teeth leave two opposing curved arcs (a bite mark).
###
The abrasion produced by a tangential force that scrapes off the superficial layer of the skin is called:
A) Pressure (crush) abrasion
B) Graze abrasion
C) Impact abrasion
D) Linear abrasion (scratch)
E) Patterned abrasion
Correct Answer: B) Graze abrasion
Explanation: A graze is produced by tangential sliding force (e.g. road rash). Pressure and impact abrasions result from force perpendicular to the skin.
###
A graze abrasion is the same as:
A) Crush abrasion
B) Pressure abrasion
C) Impact abrasion
D) Linear abrasion
E) Brush abrasion
Correct Answer: E) Brush abrasion
Explanation: "Graze" and "brush" are synonyms for a sliding, scraping abrasion. Crush, pressure and impact abrasions are caused by perpendicular force.
###
A patterned (prominence) abrasion is seen most clearly when it occurs over the:
A) Abdomen
B) Buttock
C) Scalp
D) Thigh
E) Calf
Correct Answer: C) Scalp
Explanation: The pattern prints best where skin lies tight over bone, as on the scalp. Padded areas such as the abdomen and buttock cushion the blow and blur the pattern.
###
Which of the following is NOT necessary for the formation of a patterned abrasion?
A) A soft-tissue (subcutaneous fat) cushion
B) An underlying bony base
C) Skin
D) An impact force
E) An object with a textured surface
Correct Answer: A) A soft-tissue (subcutaneous fat) cushion
Explanation: Skin over a hard bony base is needed to reproduce the pattern. Subcutaneous fat absorbs the blow and blurs the pattern.
###
Incised-like (split) lacerations can be seen in all of the following sites EXCEPT:
A) Scalp
B) Eyebrow
C) Shin
D) Chest
E) Cheekbone
Correct Answer: D) Chest
Explanation: Split lacerations occur where the skin lies directly on bone and is crushed against it. They do not occur over padded areas such as the chest wall.
###
Blunt trauma can simulate an incised wound most characteristically over which part of the body?
A) Abdomen
B) Scalp
C) Back
D) Thigh
E) Buttock
Correct Answer: B) Scalp
Explanation: The thin scalp skin is tightly stretched over the skull, so a blunt blow splits it in a clean line that looks incised. Marginal bruising, tissue bridging or crushed hair bulbs still indicate a laceration.
###
One of the following statements concerning bruises is NOT true:
A) The source of bleeding is usually small-calibre vessels such as veins, venules and arterioles
B) Bruises are caused by leakage of blood from damaged vessels into the surrounding tissues
C) The size and appearance of a bruise are related to the site of the injured part
D) Bruises result from blunt force
E) Bruises occur only in the skin and never in deeper tissues such as muscle and internal organs
Correct Answer: E) Bruises occur only in the skin and never in deeper tissues such as muscle and internal organs
Explanation: A contusion can occur anywhere in the body, for example in muscle, spleen or liver. "Bruise" usually implies a lesion visible through the skin.
###
Intradermal bruising is characterized by:
A) A deep, unpatterned haematoma
B) Pinpoint petechial haemorrhages
C) Reproduction of the pattern of the causative object, e.g. a shoe sole
D) Gravitational pooling of blood in dependent areas
E) Migration of the blood away from the site of impact
Correct Answer: C) Reproduction of the pattern of the causative object, e.g. a shoe sole
Explanation: Blood confined to the dermis cannot spread, so it keeps the pattern of the object. Hypostasis is a post-mortem phenomenon, and a deep bruise may migrate along tissue planes.
###
Which of the following is TRUE concerning lacerations?
A) They appear as cuts, splits or tears in the skin
B) They are always the result of intentional violence
C) They result from a sharp object applied to the skin
D) They never bleed profusely
E) The more resilient tissues are always divided together with the weaker tissues
Correct Answer: A) They appear as cuts, splits or tears in the skin
Explanation: Lacerations are caused by blunt force, are not always intentional (e.g. falls), and scalp lacerations can bleed profusely. Tougher tissues such as nerves and vessels survive as bridges.
###
One of the following statements regarding a lacerated wound is NOT true:
A) It is caused by blunt force
B) The margins show abrasion or contusion
C) Tissue bridging may be seen in the depth
D) Its edges are always irregular
E) It commonly occurs over bony prominences
Correct Answer: D) Its edges are always irregular
Explanation: Over a bony prominence (scalp, brow, shin) a laceration may have a deceptively regular, incised-looking edge. It is identified as a laceration by marginal bruising or abrasion and by tissue bridging.
###
Blunt weapons can usually cause all of the following EXCEPT:
A) Abrasions
B) Sharp, clean-cut edges
C) Contusions
D) Erythema
E) Oedema
Correct Answer: B) Sharp, clean-cut edges
Explanation: Clean-cut edges are the hallmark of a sharp instrument. Blunt force produces abrasions, contusions, lacerations with ragged edges, erythema and oedema.
###
One of the following is NOT a characteristic of self-inflicted injuries:
A) Superficial or minor injuries
B) Accessible sites on the body
C) Grouping on the side opposite the dominant hand
D) Tentative (hesitation) injuries
E) Usually multiple, deep and scattered wounds
Correct Answer: E) Usually multiple, deep and scattered wounds
Explanation: Self-inflicted wounds are typically superficial, tentative, grouped and within reach of the person. Multiple deep, scattered wounds point away from self-infliction.
###
Fabricated wounds are mostly:
A) Abrasions
B) Contusions
C) Incised wounds
D) Lacerations
E) Burns
Correct Answer: C) Incised wounds
Explanation: Self-made wounds produced for secondary gain are usually shallow cuts made with a sharp object on visible, reachable areas, sparing sensitive parts such as the eyes and lips.
###
What is the most frequent type of wound among defence wounds in a knife attack?
A) Incised (cut) wound
B) Laceration
C) Abrasion
D) Contusion
E) Patterned bruise
Correct Answer: A) Incised (cut) wound
Explanation: A victim who grasps at or deflects a blade sustains defence incisions, classically on the palm, dorsum of the hand and ulnar aspect of the forearm.
###
Defence wounds are most likely to be found on the:
A) Front of the neck
B) Chest
C) Lower limbs
D) Dorsum of the hands and ulnar aspect of the forearms
E) Back of the trunk
Correct Answer: D) Dorsum of the hands and ulnar aspect of the forearms
Explanation: These are the sites exposed when the victim raises the arms to ward off a blade. The palm is also involved when the blade is grasped. Defence wounds are a homicide indicator.
###
Regarding hesitation wounds, which of the following is NOT correct?
A) They are superficial
B) They are surrounded by bruises
C) They are non-lethal
D) They are parallel
E) They have regular edges
Correct Answer: B) They are surrounded by bruises
Explanation: Hesitation wounds are superficial, parallel, regular-edged incisions. Surrounding bruising implies blunt force, not a sharp-edge cut.
###
Yellow discoloration of a bruise is expected at approximately:
A) Less than 24 hours
B) 1–3 days
C) 3–5 days
D) 10–14 days
E) 5–7 days
Correct Answer: E) 5–7 days
Explanation: Yellow is due to bilirubin at about 5–7 days. Green (biliverdin) precedes it at 3–5 days, and the bruise then fades through straw to normal colour.
###
What is the usual cause of death in extradural haemorrhage?
A) Hypovolaemic shock from blood loss
B) Meningitis
C) Respiratory failure
D) Cerebral infarction
E) Primary cardiac arrhythmia
Correct Answer: C) Respiratory failure
Explanation: The expanding arterial clot raises intracranial pressure, causing tentorial herniation and brainstem (medullary) compression, which ends in respiratory failure. The haemorrhage is usually from the middle meningeal artery after a temporal bone fracture.
###
What is the main characteristic of the wound shown in the image? [img: forensic1stabwound.jpg ]
A) Irregular edges with tissue bridging
B) Marginal abrasion and contusion
C) Ragged, undermined margins
D) Clean, sharp, regular edges without surrounding bruising
E) Length greater than depth
Correct Answer: D) Clean, sharp, regular edges without surrounding bruising
Explanation: A typical stab wound has clean-cut regular edges with no marginal bruising or abrasion, and its depth exceeds its length. Irregular edges, tissue bridging and marginal bruising indicate a laceration, and a wound longer than it is deep is characteristic of an incised wound.
###
Concerning stab wounds, one of the following statements is NOT correct:
A) A slit-like wound usually distorts after removal of the weapon
B) An irregularly shaped skin wound may result if the knife is twisted
C) Movement of the knife may enlarge the wound
D) The resulting skin defect is usually exactly the size of the knife
E) One end of the wound is sharp if the weapon is single-edged
Correct Answer: D) The resulting skin defect is usually exactly the size of the knife
Explanation: Skin elasticity, Langer's lines, and rocking or twisting of the blade alter the wound, so it is rarely the exact size of the blade.
###
A fully conscious man sustained a head injury during a fight. Examination shows a boggy swelling of 6 × 4 cm over the right parietal area, and an X-ray shows a fracture of the parietal bone without displacement. The nature of the injury is:
A) Depressed fracture
B) Fissure fracture
C) Comminuted fracture
D) Compound (open) fracture
E) Complicated fracture
Correct Answer: B) Fissure fracture
Explanation: A linear crack without displacement is a fissure (linear) fracture, the commonest type. A depressed fracture has the fragment driven inward, a comminuted fracture has several fragments, and a complicated fracture involves injury to the underlying dura, brain or vessels.
###
All of the following are types of skull fracture EXCEPT:
A) Linear (fissure)
B) Depressed
C) Comminuted
D) Compound (open)
E) Spiral
Correct Answer: E) Spiral
Explanation: Skull fractures are classified as linear, depressed, comminuted, compound and complicated. A spiral pattern is a long-bone fracture.
###
A burn that destroys the full thickness of the skin, appears white or charred and leathery, and is painless because of destruction of the nerve endings is classified as:
A) First-degree
B) Second-degree
C) Third-degree
D) Fourth-degree
Correct Answer: C) Third-degree
Explanation: A third-degree burn is full-thickness skin destruction and is painless. A first-degree burn involves only the epidermis, a second-degree burn extends into the dermis with blisters and severe pain, and a fourth-degree burn extends beyond the skin into fat, muscle or bone.
###
During surgery on a patient with a gunshot wound, what is the correct approach regarding the bullet?
A) Treat the patient first, preserve or handle the bullet carefully, document its position, never discard it, and hand it to the police under a signed chain of custody
B) Remove it and discard it immediately to prevent further injury
C) Regard it as the property of the surgeon
D) Hand it to the patient's family
E) Keep it in the hospital pathology archive without notifying the police
Correct Answer: A) Treat the patient first, preserve or handle the bullet carefully, document its position, never discard it, and hand it to the police under a signed chain of custody
Explanation: The bullet is forensic evidence belonging to the police investigation. Handle it with gloved fingers or rubber-shod forceps, because metal instruments scratch the rifling marks, and place it in a labelled, sealed container.
