Hesitation marks are seen in:
A) Homicidal attempts
B) Accidental injury
C) Suicidal attempts
D) Defense wounds
Correct Answer: C) Suicidal attempts
Explanation: Hesitation marks are multiple shallow, parallel test cuts made before the fatal incision — characteristic of suicidal cut-throat/wrist injuries.
###
In suicidal cut throats, we see:
A) Hesitation marks
B) Multiple deep stab wounds
C) Incised wounds on the back
D) No pattern
Correct Answer: A) Hesitation marks
Explanation: Suicidal cut-throats show progressive hesitation marks reflecting the "testing" phase before the fatal incision, unlike homicidal single deep wounds.
###
Green color in a bruise is due to:
A) Hemosiderin
B) Bilirubin
C) Oxyhemoglobin
D) Biliverdin
Correct Answer: D) Biliverdin
Explanation: Biliverdin production (~3–5 days post-injury) gives bruises their green color, following the red/blue → brown progression.
###
Which is FALSE about abrasions?
A) They are superficial wounds
B) Third healing stage involves granulation tissue
C) They indicate the direction of force
D) They heal without scarring
Correct Answer: B) Third healing stage involves granulation tissue
Explanation: Abrasions involve only the epidermis and heal by re-epithelialization, not granulation tissue — granulation is a deeper-wound healing feature, making this statement false.
###
True about abrasion:
A) Even if it bleeds, it heals WITHOUT scarring
B) Always leaves a scar
C) Cannot be dated
D) Only caused by blunt objects
Correct Answer: A) Even if it bleeds, it heals WITHOUT scarring
Explanation: Because abrasions are epidermal-only injuries, they heal by re-epithelialization without significant scarring even if superficial bleeding occurs.
###
The best type of injury for indicating the tool used:
A) Laceration
B) Cut wound
C) Abrasions
Correct Answer: C) Abrasions
Explanation: Patterned abrasions best reproduce the surface contour of the inflicting object (e.g., tyre marks, buckle imprints), making abrasions the most useful for tool identification.
###
Importance of Langer's lines (Lines of Langerhans):
A) Gaping of wounds
B) Direction of wound healing
C) Infection risk assessment
D) Pattern identification
Correct Answer: A) Gaping of wounds
Explanation: Langer's lines reflect skin tension/collagen orientation; wounds parallel to them gape less, while perpendicular wounds gape widely.
###
Clinical importance of Langer's lines in a wound:
A) Determines the depth of the wound
B) Determines the direction of hair growth
C) Predicts infection risk
D) Determines the degree of gaping of an incised wound
Correct Answer: D) Determines the degree of gaping of an incised wound
Explanation: The clinical value of Langer's lines is predicting the degree of gaping of an incised wound based on its orientation relative to skin tension lines.
###
Crescent-shaped abrasions are caused by:
A) Blunt instrument
B) Fingernail
C) Teeth
D) Ring
Correct Answer: B) Fingernail
Explanation: Fingernails produce characteristic crescent (semi-lunar) shaped abrasions, distinct from the paired arcuate marks of teeth.
###
The superficial layer of skin scraped off (tangential force) is called:
A) Pressure abrasion
B) Graze abrasion
C) Friction abrasion
D) Impact abrasion
Correct Answer: B) Graze abrasion
Explanation: A graze (brush) abrasion results from tangential force scraping off the superficial epidermis, as in road-rash type injuries.
###
Graze abrasion is the same as:
A) Punch abrasion
B) Friction abrasion
C) Brush abrasion
D) Pressure abrasion
Correct Answer: C) Brush abrasion
Explanation: "Graze" and "brush" abrasion are synonymous terms for the same tangential scraping mechanism.
###
Prominence abrasion is most commonly found in which area of the body?
A) Scalp
B) Face
C) Chest
D) Back
Correct Answer: A) Scalp
Explanation: Over the scalp, skin is tightly applied to the underlying bone with minimal soft-tissue cushioning, allowing the clearest reproduction of a patterned abrasion.
###
Patterned abrasion looks clearest when the injury is on the:
A) Abdomen
B) Back
C) Forearm
D) Scalp
Correct Answer: D) Scalp
Explanation: Bony prominences like the scalp lack a soft-tissue cushion, so the weapon's surface pattern is reproduced most clearly there.
###
NOT necessary to see a patterned abrasion:
A) Bone underneath
B) Soft tissue (subcutaneous fat)
C) Skin
Correct Answer: B) Soft tissue (subcutaneous fat)
Explanation: Subcutaneous fat cushions the blow and distorts the pattern, so its absence (not its presence) is what favors clear patterned abrasions — bone underneath is what's required.
###
Incised-like lacerations can be seen in all of the following EXCEPT:
A) Scalp
B) Eyebrow
C) Chest
D) Shin
Correct Answer: C) Chest
Explanation: Incised-like lacerations occur where skin is crushed directly against underlying bone (scalp, eyebrow, shin); the chest wall has more soft-tissue cushioning, preventing this clean-split appearance.
###
Blunt trauma can simulate an incised wound specifically over which part of the body:
A) Abdomen
B) Back
C) Scalp (over bony prominences)
D) Thighs
Correct Answer: C) Scalp (over bony prominences)
Explanation: Blunt force over the thin, bone-backed scalp can split the skin cleanly enough to mimic an incised wound.
###
One of the following is NOT TRUE concerning bruises:
A) Bruising is most commonly seen in the skin, but it NEVER occurs in the deeper tissues, including muscle and internal organs
B) The source of bleeding is usually small-caliber blood vessels such as veins and arterioles
C) Bruises are caused by leakage of blood into underlying tissues from damaged blood vessels
D) The size and appearance of a bruise is related to the site of the injured part of the body
E) Bruises are discoloration of the skin surface due to a blunt force instrument
Correct Answer: A) Bruising is most commonly seen in the skin, but it NEVER occurs in the deeper tissues, including muscle and internal organs
Explanation: Bruising is not confined to skin — it can and does occur in deep tissues, muscle, and internal organs (e.g., deep haematomas, organ contusions), making this statement false.
###
Intradermal bruising is best described as:
A) Deep bruise
B) Petechial hemorrhage
C) Hypostasis
D) Patterned bruise
Correct Answer: D) Patterned bruise
Explanation: Intradermal bruising traps blood within the dermis, reproducing the pattern of the inflicting object — the most forensically informative type of bruise.
###
One of the following is TRUE concerning laceration:
A) Laceration is always connected to intentional violent acts
B) Lacerations appear as cuts, splits or tears in the skin
C) The more resilient tissues will always be damaged by the forces that split the other weaker tissues
D) They appear as the result of a sharp object against skin
E) They never bleed profusely
Correct Answer: B) Lacerations appear as cuts, splits or tears in the skin
Explanation: Lacerations are defined as tears/splits in the skin from blunt force, with irregular edges and surrounding bruising — not from sharp objects.
###
NOT true regarding a lacerated wound:
A) Edges are irregular
B) Associated with bruising
C) Caused by blunt force
D) Always irregular
Correct Answer: D) Always irregular
Explanation: While lacerations are usually irregular, those over bony prominences (scalp, shin) can appear deceptively regular/incised-like, so "always irregular" is not strictly true.
###
Blunt instruments/weapons can usually cause all of the following EXCEPT:
A) Abrasions
B) Erythema
C) Sharp cut edges
D) Contusion
E) Edema
Correct Answer: C) Sharp cut edges
Explanation: Sharp, clean-cut edges are a feature of sharp-weapon injuries, not blunt trauma, which instead produces abrasions, contusions, and ragged lacerations.
###
One of the following is NOT characteristic of self-inflicted injuries:
A) Usually multiple and deep wounds
B) Superficial or minor injury
C) Injuries are grouped on the contralateral side to the patient's common hand use
D) On an area of the body that the individual can access themselves
E) Tentative injuries
Correct Answer: A) Usually multiple and deep wounds
Explanation: Self-inflicted wounds are typically superficial and tentative (hesitation marks); multiple deep wounds instead suggest homicidal or accidental injury.
###
Fabricated wounds are mostly:
A) Abrasions
B) Contusions
C) Lacerations
D) Incised wounds
Correct Answer: D) Incised wounds
Explanation: Fabricated (self-inflicted for secondary gain) wounds are usually incised, made with a sharp object on visible, accessible areas.
###
Which is the most frequent type of wound seen in DEFENSE wounds:
A) Abrasion
B) Cut wound
C) Laceration
D) Contusion
Correct Answer: B) Cut wound
Explanation: Defense wounds are most often cut/incised injuries sustained when the victim grabs or deflects a blade.
###
Defense wounds are most likely seen in:
A) Back of hands / dorsum of forearms
B) Self-inflicted cut throat
C) Chest
D) Lower limbs
Correct Answer: A) Back of hands / dorsum of forearms
Explanation: Classic defense wound sites are the ulnar border and dorsum of the forearms/hands, raised instinctively to shield the face and neck.
###
Hesitation wound – which is NOT correct:
A) Superficial
B) Not lethal
C) Surrounded by bruises
D) Parallel
E) Regular edges
Correct Answer: C) Surrounded by bruises
Explanation: Hesitation marks are superficial, parallel, regular-edged tentative cuts — they are not surrounded by bruising, which would instead suggest blunt trauma.
###
Yellow discoloration of a bruise is expected at approximately what time?
A) 5–7 days
B) 1–2 days
C) 3–5 days
D) 10–14 days
Correct Answer: A) 5–7 days
Explanation: Bilirubin formation from degraded haemoglobin typically produces the yellow discoloration of a bruise around 5–7 days post-injury.
###
Common cause of death in extradural hemorrhage:
A) Hemorrhagic shock
B) Cardiac arrest
C) Sepsis
D) Respiratory failure
E) Renal failure
Correct Answer: D) Respiratory failure
Explanation: Extradural hemorrhage causes rapid mass effect and brainstem herniation, leading to death primarily from respiratory centre failure.
###
What is the main characteristic of the wound shown in the image? [img: forensic1stabwound.jpg ]
A) Ragged, irregular edges with tissue bridging
B) Sharp, clean edges with regular margins and no surrounding bruising
C) Diffuse bruising with poorly defined margins
D) Multiple parallel superficial hesitation cuts

###
Concerning stab wounds, one of the following statements is NOT correct:
A) A slit-like wound will usually distort after removal of the weapon
B) An irregularly-shaped skin wound may result if the knife is twisted
C) Movement of the knife may cause wound enlargement
D) One wound apex is sharp if the weapon is single sharp-edged
E) The resulting defect of the edges is usually the exact size as the knife
Correct Answer: E) The resulting defect of the edges is usually the exact size as the knife
Explanation: Skin elasticity, wound-edge retraction, and any blade movement mean the resulting skin defect is rarely the exact size of the knife, even when edges are approximated.
###
A fully conscious person sustained a head injury during a fight. Boggy swelling 6x4 cm on the right parietal area. X-ray showed a fracture of the parietal bone without displacement. The nature of the injury is:
A) Depressed Fracture
B) Open wound Fracture
C) Fissure fracture
D) Comminuted fracture
E) Complicated fracture
Correct Answer: C) Fissure fracture
Explanation: A single linear fracture without bone displacement is classified as a fissure (linear) fracture, distinct from depressed or comminuted patterns.
###
A skull fracture with multiple bone fragments is termed:
A) Comminuted (fragmented) fracture
B) Linear (fissure) fracture
C) Greenstick fracture
D) Compression fracture
Correct Answer: A) Comminuted (fragmented) fracture
Explanation: A comminuted (fragmented) skull fracture is defined by multiple bone fragments, as opposed to a single linear fissure line.
###
A burn that is full-thickness and painless due to nerve destruction is classified as:
A) 1st degree
B) 2nd degree
C) 4th degree
D) 3rd degree
Correct Answer: D) 3rd degree
Explanation: Full-thickness skin destruction with loss of sensation (due to nerve destruction) defines a 3rd-degree burn, distinguishing it from the painful, blistering 2nd-degree burn.
###
In a gunshot wound case during surgery, the correct approach regarding the bullet is:
A) Remove and discard the bullet immediately to prevent further injury
B) Treat the wound; preserve the bullet in situ if possible; document; do NOT discard it
C) The bullet is the surgeon's property
D) The bullet must be handed to the patient's family
Correct Answer: B) Treat the wound; preserve the bullet in situ if possible; document; do NOT discard it
Explanation: A retrieved bullet is critical ballistic evidence — it must be preserved in situ where possible, handled carefully, documented, and never discarded.
