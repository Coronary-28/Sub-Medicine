Which of the following cases is considered a medico-legal case?
A) Death at home from a known chronic natural disease
B) Death in hospital from a known disease under medical care
C) Death of a person in prison
D) Expected death of a terminally ill patient under palliative care
E) Death of an elderly patient from known heart failure attended by his physician
Correct Answer: C) Death of a person in prison
Explanation: Any death in custody (prison, police, juvenile home) is a medico-legal case regardless of its apparent cause, to exclude foul play and establish accountability. The other options are expected natural deaths under medical care.

Which of the following is a cause of myocardial infarction without atheroma?
A) Intense emotional stress
B) Uncontrolled hypertension
C) Hyperlipidaemia
D) Uncontrolled diabetes mellitus
E) Advanced age
Correct Answer: A) Intense emotional stress
Explanation: Intense emotional stress causes coronary spasm through a catecholamine surge and can produce infarction in the absence of atheroma. Hypertension, hyperlipidaemia and diabetes act through atheroma formation.
###
A prisoner dies from bronchospasm. Which of the following statements is correct?
A) A standard death certificate is sufficient
B) An autopsy is not required because the cause of death is natural
C) Family consent is required before an autopsy can be performed
D) The death must be reported by law to the public prosecutor
E) The prison physician may release the body for burial without notification
Correct Answer: D) The death must be reported by law to the public prosecutor
Explanation: A death in custody is a mandatory medico-legal case, so it must be reported to the public prosecutor and followed by a medico-legal autopsy. Neither a death certificate alone nor family consent is applicable, even if the apparent cause is natural.
###
Which of the following causes of death is NOT considered medico-legal?
A) Pulmonary embolism following a hip fracture in a hospitalized patient
B) Tuberculosis in a 30-year-old refugee under medical care
C) Pneumonia in a 16-year-old male in a juvenile home
D) A 30-year-old brought in dead following a head injury
E) A 60-year-old man who died suddenly while waiting for an X-ray
Correct Answer: B) Tuberculosis in a 30-year-old refugee under medical care
Explanation: Death from a diagnosed disease under active medical care is an expected natural death. The other options involve trauma or its complications, custody, a "brought in dead" case, or sudden unexpected death.
###
When a forensic physician is called to the scene by the public prosecutor, the primary purposes are:
A) Only to confirm that death has occurred
B) Only to collect toxicology samples
C) To clean and document the scene
D) To perform a complete autopsy at the scene
E) To identify the deceased and investigate the circumstances of death
Correct Answer: E) To identify the deceased and investigate the circumstances of death
Explanation: The forensic physician identifies the deceased, determines the time, cause and manner of death, and helps reconstruct the events for the prosecutor. Confirming death or collecting samples are only parts of this role.
###
Which stage of lobar pneumonia is most likely to cause death in children?
A) Stage of resolution
B) Stage of red hepatization
C) Stage of congestion (first stage)
D) Stage of grey hepatization
Correct Answer: C) Stage of congestion (first stage)
Explanation: Children most often die in the first stage, from overwhelming toxaemia, before consolidation develops. Red and grey hepatization are later stages, and resolution is the recovery stage.
###
What is the most common cause of sudden unexpected death?
A) Cardiovascular disease
B) Respiratory disease
C) Central nervous system disease
D) Metabolic disease
E) Gastrointestinal disease
Correct Answer: A) Cardiovascular disease
Explanation: Coronary artery disease with myocardial infarction, together with hypertensive heart disease, accounts for most sudden unexpected deaths in adults. Respiratory, CNS, metabolic and gastrointestinal causes are far less frequent.
