Activated charcoal is useless in all of the following EXCEPT:
A) Cyanide
B) Iron
C) Salicylate ingestion
D) Lithium
Correct Answer: C) Salicylate ingestion
Explanation: Activated charcoal effectively adsorbs salicylates (along with paracetamol and tricyclics), but it is ineffective for iron, lithium, cyanide, corrosives, alcohols, and heavy metals.
###
Which of the following substances is best absorbed (adsorbed) by activated charcoal?
A) Salicylic acid
B) Iron
C) Lithium
D) Cyanide
Correct Answer: A) Salicylic acid
Explanation: Salicylate is well adsorbed by activated charcoal, whereas iron, lithium, and cyanide are not effectively bound by it.
###
Which statement about activated charcoal is TRUE?
A) It reduces poison absorption by adsorbing it in the GI lumen
B) It works by absorption into the toxin
C) It induces vomiting
D) It is contraindicated in all cases of poisoning
Correct Answer: A) It reduces poison absorption by adsorbing it in the GI lumen
Explanation: Activated charcoal works via adsorption — binding the toxin to its surface in the gut — thereby preventing systemic absorption, rather than being absorbed itself or inducing emesis.
###
Which of the following statements about activated charcoal is FALSE?
A) It should be given as soon as possible after ingestion
B) It can be combined with syrup of ipecac to induce vomiting
C) It works by adsorbing toxins in the gut
D) It is ineffective for metals and alcohols
Correct Answer: B) It can be combined with syrup of ipecac to induce vomiting
Explanation: Activated charcoal and syrup of ipecac should never be combined, since ipecac-induced vomiting would expel the charcoal from the stomach before it can adsorb the toxin.
###
All of the following are true about activated charcoal EXCEPT:
A) Give with a sufficient amount of water
B) It is a finely arranged black powder
C) Given with syrup of ipecac to increase effectiveness
D) Used for many poisons
Correct Answer: C) Given with syrup of ipecac to increase effectiveness
Explanation: Ipecac is not routinely recommended and should never be combined with activated charcoal, as it would counteract the charcoal's effect by inducing vomiting rather than improving efficacy.
###
A patient arrives at the ER 2 hours after an overdose. What is the correct management?
A) Activated charcoal if no contraindications
B) Syrup of ipecac
C) Gastric lavage
D) All of the above
Correct Answer: A) Activated charcoal if no contraindications
Explanation: Within 2 hours of ingestion, activated charcoal is first-line if the airway is intact and there are no contraindications; syrup of ipecac is no longer recommended, and gastric lavage is rarely used (limited to within about 1 hour with a protected airway).

Why are alkaline (base) burns generally MORE dangerous than acidic burns?
A) They cause liquefactive necrosis, allowing deeper tissue penetration
B) They cause coagulative necrosis, which limits penetration
C) Acids are neutralized immediately upon contact
D) Bases do not penetrate mucous membranes
Correct Answer: A) They cause liquefactive necrosis, allowing deeper tissue penetration
Explanation: Alkalis dissolve proteins and saponify fat, producing liquefactive necrosis without a limiting eschar, allowing progressively deeper tissue injury; acids instead cause coagulative necrosis, which forms a protective eschar that limits further penetration.
