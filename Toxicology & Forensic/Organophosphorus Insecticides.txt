Which specific enzyme is reactivated after organophosphate poisoning (before aging)?
A) Acetylcholinesterase (AChE) — reactivated by Pralidoxime
B) Monoamine oxidase
C) Butyrylcholinesterase only
D) Atropinase
Correct Answer: A) Acetylcholinesterase (AChE) — reactivated by Pralidoxime
Explanation: OP poisons phosphorylate AChE; before "aging" occurs (~48h), pralidoxime can remove the phosphoryl group and restore enzyme activity.
###
The drug that reactivates phosphorylated AChE in organophosphate poisoning (before aging):
A) Atropine
B) Diazepam
C) Pralidoxime (2-PAM)
D) Physostigmine
Correct Answer: C) Pralidoxime (2-PAM)
Explanation: Pralidoxime is an oxime AChE reactivator effective only before aging; atropine blocks muscarinic effects but does not reactivate the enzyme.
###
Enzyme reactivated by pralidoxime in organophosphate poisoning:
A) Acetylcholinesterase (AChE)
B) Monoamine oxidase
C) Pseudocholinesterase
D) Cytochrome P450
Correct Answer: A) Acetylcholinesterase (AChE)
Explanation: Pralidoxime specifically reactivates AChE (not pseudocholinesterase/BuChE), and only before the phosphorylated enzyme ages.
###
Which of the following statements about carbamate and organophosphate poisoning is FALSE?
A) Both cause muscarinic and nicotinic symptoms
B) Atropine is used for symptomatic treatment
C) Both can lead to respiratory failure
D) Both are treated with pralidoxime
Correct Answer: D) Both are treated with pralidoxime
Explanation: Pralidoxime is not indicated for carbamate poisoning because carbamate-enzyme inhibition does not undergo aging (it's spontaneously reversible), unlike OP poisoning.
###
Management of carbamate toxicity – all of the following EXCEPT:
A) Protect airway
B) Starting treatment should be delayed until level of anti-cholinesterase is known
C) Remove clothes
D) Atropine
Correct Answer: B) Starting treatment should be delayed until level of anti-cholinesterase is known
Explanation: Treatment (ABC + atropine) must never be delayed for lab confirmation in cholinesterase-inhibitor poisoning, as this is a time-critical emergency.
###
Not a muscarinic sign of organophosphate poisoning:
A) Urinary incontinence
B) Urinary retention
C) Anuria
D) Oliguria
Correct Answer: B) Urinary retention
Explanation: Muscarinic (SLUDGE) effects cause urinary incontinence/frequency; urinary retention instead reflects a nicotinic/sympathetic effect.
###
What is the role of atropine in the treatment of organophosphate poisoning?
A) Block nicotinic effects
B) Neutralize the poison
C) Block muscarinic effects
D) Promote toxin elimination
Correct Answer: C) Block muscarinic effects
Explanation: Atropine is a competitive muscarinic antagonist that dries secretions and reverses muscarinic toxicity; it has no effect on nicotinic weakness, which requires pralidoxime.
###
Management and treatment of organophosphate poisoning – which is TRUE:
A) Suctioning of secretions
B) Start with antidote (pralidoxime)
C) Atropine is antidote for nicotinic receptor
D) Delay until laboratory tests shown
Correct Answer: A) Suctioning of secretions
Explanation: Initial priority is airway management including suctioning of bronchial secretions, followed by atropine for muscarinic symptoms and then pralidoxime.
###
Low dose of organophosphates is NOT associated with:
A) Increased bronchial secretion
B) Miosis
C) Salivation
D) Paralysis
Correct Answer: D) Paralysis
Explanation: Low-dose OP exposure produces muscarinic effects (bronchorrhea, miosis, salivation); paralysis is a nicotinic effect seen at high dose.
###
Organophosphate-induced neuropsychiatric disorder (OPIDP) happens:
A) Immediately
B) After 1-5 weeks of OP poisoning
C) Within 24 hours
D) After 6 months
Correct Answer: B) After 1-5 weeks of OP poisoning
Explanation: OPIDP is a delayed peripheral polyneuropathy appearing 1–5 weeks post-exposure, distinct from the immediate cholinergic crisis and the 24–96h intermediate syndrome.
###
Antidote of organophosphate poisoning:
A) Naloxone
B) N-Acetylcysteine
C) Atropine + Pralidoxime
D) Flumazenil
Correct Answer: C) Atropine + Pralidoxime
Explanation: Dual therapy is required — atropine blocks muscarinic toxicity while pralidoxime reactivates AChE, addressing nicotinic and central effects.
